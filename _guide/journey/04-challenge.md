# Step 04 — Challenge (critical about the why, positive about the how)

**Goal:** turn a wish list into a short, honest priority list, so the person builds what really helps and avoids building what would only add work.
**Time:** 10–20 minutes.
**Output:** ideas sorted into **Now / Next / Later**.

---

## Your stance

- Be a sparring partner, not a cheerleader and not a gatekeeper.
- **Critical** about whether something is needed, how often it matters, and whether it's the simplest version.
- **Positive** about feasibility: if they want it, there's usually a way. Don't stop at "that's not possible": look for another route, and be honest if one is truly blocked.
- The person decides. If they keep something you would drop, say why once and move on.

## 1. List the candidate ideas

Combine:
- the ideas that caught their attention in step 02;
- the pains and recurring work from step 03 (each pain is a potential flow);
- anything else they mention now.

Write them as short lines: "When X happens, Y should be done for me."

## 2. Ask the useful questions, idea by idea

Pick the ones that apply; don't run every question on every idea.

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| "How often does this happen?" | A daily problem beats a yearly one |
| "What does it cost you today (time, stress, money, missed chances)?" | Value |
| "What's the simplest version that would already help?" | Avoid overbuilding |
| "Could a tool you already have do it (a filter, a calendar subscription, a template)?" | Sometimes no AI is needed |
| "What would you do with the result?" | If nothing, it's collecting, not helping |
| "What happens if it goes wrong once?" | Risk, and how much checking is needed |
| "Does it depend on another flow existing first?" | Order |

Share your honest view briefly, e.g., "This one looks high value and simple. This other one is exciting but depends on three others; I'd keep it for later."

## 3. Watch for common traps (mention them only when relevant)

- **Building everything at once.** The most common reason second brains are abandoned. Imagine big, build small.
- **Collecting without using.** Saving everything feels productive but helps only if something comes out of it.
- **Too much structure up front.** Folders and categories should appear when a flow needs them.
- **Automating something done once a month in five minutes.** Not always worth it, unless it removes stress.
- **Costs creeping up.** Each automation that runs often adds cost; prefer included or free options first.

## 4. Sort into Now / Next / Later

Propose a sorting, then let them adjust:

- **Now (1 idea, maybe 2):** high value, frequent, simple, low risk, uses tools they already have.
- **Next (2–4):** valuable but needs the first one, or more setup.
- **Later:** everything else. Nothing is lost; it stays on the map as "planned".

A good first flow is usually: inbox triage, voice capture, a daily or weekly summary, deadlines into a calendar, or meeting notes into people files. But the person chooses.

## 5. Save and move on

1. Update `_system/PROGRESS.md`: step 04 ticked; key answers: the Now / Next / Later lists.
2. Add the Later ideas to `_system/BACKLOG.md` under "Ideas for later".

Transition: "Now comes the most important step: drawing your second brain by hand. It takes 15–30 minutes and makes everything that follows much clearer."
