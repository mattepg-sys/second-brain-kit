# Step 05 — Draw your map (strongly recommended)

**Goal:** the person stops, sits down with paper and pen, and draws the second brain they want: places and the flows between them. This is the moment they decide what they really want, before any AI builds anything.
**Time:** 15–30 minutes away from the screen.
**Output:** a photo (or a description) of their hand-drawn map.

---

## 1. Explain why it matters (short)

Say, in your own words:
- Drawing by hand forces clear thinking about what goes where and why.
- It prevents the AI (me) from building things you don't need.
- It doesn't need to be pretty. Messy is fine.
- **It doesn't need to be complete or complex.** Draw what you know today: three boxes and two arrows is a great map. It will grow, and it can change at any time.

### Show an example first, right here in the chat
People draw much more easily after seeing one. Don't just point to a file:
- remind them of **Alex's map** from step 02. If you can show images or diagrams in the chat, show it (or a simplified version); otherwise describe it in 4–5 lines from `_guide/example-alex/alex-story.md`;
- tell the short story of how it grew: **Alex started with one arrow** (voice notes → inbox → sorted into projects), used it for a couple of weeks, then added the next; the big map came months later;
- show one or two **tiny example drawings** as text, adapted to the person's life, for example:

```
 [Voice notes] --every evening, AI--> [Inbox] --I check--> [Projects]
 [University portal] --automatic--> [Calendar] --Mondays, AI--> [Weekly plan]
```

If the person prefers not to draw, respect it and use the alternatives in section 4. Recommend it once, warmly; don't insist.

## 2. Give the drawing instructions

Share these clearly (adapt the wording):

1. **Take a sheet of paper** (landscape is easier).
2. **Draw boxes for places.** A place is anywhere information lives:
   - where it **comes from** (email, voice notes, meetings, a university portal, the bank…), usually on the left;
   - where it **lands** (a project folder, People, Health…), in the middle;
   - where it **goes out** (a draft email, a calendar, a PDF to read, a weekly summary…), on the right.
   You can add more columns: an output can become the input of another flow. There are no fixed levels.
3. **Draw arrows for flows.** Each arrow moves something from one place to another.
4. **Label each arrow with up to four things** (it's fine to write "?"):
   - **what** moves ("deadlines", "facts and decisions", "receipts");
   - **when** ("every morning", "when a file arrives", "when I ask");
   - **who** does it ("AI", "Zapier", "?");
   - **check** ("I approve", "I just read it", "none").
5. **Star ★** the arrows that matter most.
6. **Circle ◯** the one arrow you'd like to build first (it can match the "Now" idea from step 04, or not).
7. **Take a photo** and upload it here.

Tell them they can include today's flows and the dream ones: the map is the destination, not the first step.

## 3. While they draw

If you are in an ongoing chat, say you'll wait. If they come back later, `PROGRESS.md` will tell you to continue from here.

## 4. Alternatives (if they can't or don't want to draw)

- **Describe it:** they write or dictate it as lines like "Gmail → every morning → AI → list of emails that need me → I read it".
- **Interview:** you ask, place by place: "Where does information come from? Where should it end up? What should happen in between?" and build the list together.
- **Table:** they fill in a simple table: From | To | What | When | Who | Check.

If you can't see images, ask them to describe the photo or use one of these alternatives.

## 5. Save and move on

Update `_system/PROGRESS.md`: step 05 ticked (or "described in text"); note that the map was received.
Save the map so later sessions can see it: the photo as `_system/map-sketch-YYYY-MM-DD.jpg`, or a text description as `_system/map-sketch-YYYY-MM-DD.md`. In chat mode, put a short version of the description in `PROGRESS.md` under "Key answers".

Transition: "Thanks! Let me read your map carefully and give you feedback."
