# Step 02 — Discover what is possible

**Goal:** help the person imagine what their second brain could become, with concrete examples, before talking about their own needs.
**Time:** 10–15 minutes.
**Output:** a short list of ideas that caught their attention.

---

## 1. Explain the idea in 4 sentences

In your own words:

- A second brain has **places** (where information comes from, where it lands, where it goes out) and **flows** (arrows that move information between places).
- Each flow has four labels: **what** moves, **when**, **who** does it (an AI, an automation tool, a script) and the **checkpoint** (what you check or approve).
- Some flows run on their own, for example every morning before you wake up; others run when you ask.
- The power comes from flows that share **context**: your profile, your projects, the people you know. That's what makes the outputs useful instead of generic.

## 2. Show Alex's map

Alex is a fictional founder who is also a university student and a parent. His second brain grew over several months, one flow at a time.

- **File mode:** tell the person to open `_guide/example-alex/alex-map.html` in their browser (double-click it). Give them a minute.
- **Chat mode or no browser:** describe the map using `_guide/example-alex/alex-story.md`.
- The map's labels are in English. If the person doesn't read English, describe it in their language as they look at it.

Read `alex-story.md` yourself so you can explain the map.

## 3. Walk through 4–5 example flows

Choose examples that match anything the person has already mentioned; otherwise pick one from each area. For each, give one line of what it does and one line of why it helps. Keep it concrete, for example:

| Area | Example flow (from Alex) | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | Voice notes recorded while walking become text in the inbox | Ideas are never lost |
| Morning | At 06:00, everything dropped in the inbox is sorted into the right folders, with a short summary | You start the day with order, not a pile |
| Email | Each morning, a list of only the emails that need you, with draft replies ready | Less time in the inbox |
| People | After a meeting, the people you met get a note with what you discussed and what you promised | You never forget a follow-up |
| Study | University deadlines flow into a weekly study plan | No missed deadlines |
| Family | School emails become calendar events and to-dos | Less mental load |
| Money | Each month, bank exports become a one-page spending summary | Clarity without spreadsheets |
| Health | Lab results (PDFs) become a timeline of values over time | Better conversations with doctors |
| System | A daily check flags only what the system can't fix by itself | You trust your second brain |

More ideas are in `_guide/library/ideas-catalog.md`. Don't read them all out; use it to answer "what else could it do?".

## 4. Be honest about growth and costs

Say briefly:
- Alex didn't build this in a weekend. It grew one flow at a time, keeping only what proved useful.
- Many flows are free or included in a normal AI subscription. Others cost money, and **costs grow with the number of flows and how often they run**. (A founder with dozens of heavy daily automations reported a month costing around €1,000 in AI usage; a student with a few flows can stay close to free.) You'll always see the cost direction before building anything.

## 5. Ask what caught their attention

"Which of these made you think 'I want that'? Anything you'd love that you didn't see?"

Listen. Note their reactions. Don't start designing yet.

## 6. Save and move on

Update `_system/PROGRESS.md`: step 02 ticked; under "Key answers", the ideas that caught their attention in their words. Next step: `03-your-story.md`.

Transition: "Now let's talk about you: your projects, your days, and where information gets lost."
